---
title: "Risk and protection"
description: "Margin, shared-account risk and policy-defined protection."
---

Cinder checks both your individual margin and the resources backing the shared venue account. Opposing customer positions can reduce the venue's net position without eliminating either customer's exposure. Pending orders, available house capital and payment liquidity also constrain new activity.

Enough assets in total does not always mean enough immediately accessible cash for a withdrawal.

## Protection mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it does | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin and admission checks | Limit new positions and commitments against user and pool resources. | Market shocks and venue actions can still breach a limit. |
| Risk separation | Keeps exposure in defined risk units rather than treating every venue account as interchangeable. | Users in the same native margin account remain exposed to some shared venue actions. |
| House protection fund | Pays eligible losses under the applicable coverage policy. | Coverage, claim priority and available capital are finite; this is not blanket insurance. |
| ADL restoration | Attempts an authorized, capital-backed replacement after a venue reduces Cinder's position. | Restoration depends on execution, liquidity and the approved budget. |
| Proportional reduction | Allocates affected quantity that cannot be restored among eligible same-side positions. | An otherwise healthy private position can still be reduced. |

Customer collateral and Cinder's loss-bearing capital are accounted for separately. Your ordinary trading loss is not automatically charged to other customers. Fund claims are also distinct from paid claims: an unpaid obligation does not disappear because protection capital is exhausted.

The **house protection fund** is Cinder's loss-bearing capital, not a second name for customer deposits. Whether a particular loss is covered, and how much can be paid, depends on the applicable coverage policy and available resources. The presence of a fund is not a promise to reimburse every loss.

## Risk units and ADL

A **risk unit** is the group of positions and resources affected by the same venue account's margin and closeout rules. A failure in one unit is not automatically spread to unrelated units.

Liquidation enforces margin requirements. **Auto-deleveraging (ADL)** is a venue-driven reduction of exposure that can affect a position without that customer failing their own margin check.

Venue ADL can reduce Cinder's aggregate position even when some individual customers are healthy. **Restoration** means attempting a permitted replacement of the removed exposure. Cinder first applies the account's restoration policy. For an authorized replacement that executes, original entry economics are preserved and the replacement-price difference and execution cost are assigned to Cinder's house resources.

If affected quantity remains unrestored, it is reduced proportionally among eligible positions on the affected side, within that risk unit. Allocation uses whole lots and the policy's deterministic rounding rule.

### Example

Alice is long 6 lots and Bob is long 4 lots in the same affected group. After any restoration, 5 lots still need to be reduced.

Alice loses 3 lots and Bob loses 2 lots: each position is reduced by 50%. Their remaining positions are 3 and 2 lots. This reduction does not mean either customer breached their individual margin requirement.

## What protection does not promise

Cinder does not guarantee that every healthy position survives a shared-account event, that every venue loss is covered, or that pooled treatment always matches a separate native account.

Available coverage and limits follow the policy applicable to your account. Fees follow the venue and Cinder's stated fee policy; they are not a guarantee of the lowest fee in every circumstance.

title: "Privacy"
description: "Who can see your account data—and what remains visible."
---

Your SDK verifies the enclave's identity and encrypts private requests before they cross Cinder's relay infrastructure. Decryption and account processing happen inside the confidential enclave.

## Privacy by observer

| Observer | Visible information |
| --- | --- |
| Public blockchain | Deposits, withdrawals, recovery claims and their transfer details; public recovery commitments. Not your complete private balance, positions or order history. |
| Execution venue | Cinder's shared account, aggregate orders, positions and funding activity. Not Cinder's private user-level allocation records. |
| Parent instance and relay infrastructure | Encrypted messages, routing handles and traffic metadata such as timing and sizes. Not the plaintext of signed private commands or replies. |
| Account owner | Their own balances, positions, orders and history. |
| Authorized agent | Information and actions allowed by its grant. READ includes account information across markets. |

Privacy does not mean anonymous transfers or invisible network activity. Traffic patterns may reveal information even when message contents are encrypted.

## Client and device security

Use SDK code and the expected enclave release identity from a trusted distribution channel. A compromised website, wallet integration or device can read your data before encryption or after decryption.

Closing a session releases its encryption objects, but does not guarantee that every copy of a secret has immediately disappeared from browser or device memory. Local device compromise is therefore outside the protection offered by encrypted transport.

Attestation identifies the measured code and channel key; it is not a guarantee that the code has no vulnerabilities. See [verification](/security/verification) for the scope of each security check.
